## PART 1 - THE FULL LIFECYCLE, STEP BY STEP (10 steps, 2 phases)

### PHASE A - SET-UP (before any truck moves)

| Step | What happens | The rule / proof |
|---|---|---|
| **A1. National law** | The LGAs must manage solid waste; NEMC issues permits to collect/transport; vehicle standards apply | EMA 2004 (Act No. 20) + GN 263/2009 Regs 9 & 10 - `laws-and-regulations/` |
| **A2. District by-laws** | Each council passes its own refuse by-laws: DCC 1994 (first), Kinondoni 2000 (became the national template), Temeke GN 45/2001 gazette | CSE 2021; Temeke by-laws PDF in `districts/04_TEMEKE/pdf/` |
| **A3. Zoning + tender** | Council divides the city into franchise zones and advertises a tender in the official Procurement Journal (e.g. Kinondoni LGA017/2020-21, 8 collection lots; DCC "Integrated Solid Waste Management" EoI; Temeke Mtoni landfill leasing) | PPRA TPJ editions Ed 07/14/29 in `sample-documents/`; Kinondoni tender PDF in `districts/02_KINONDONI/pdf/` |
| **A4. Contract signed** | Franchise/service contract, duration **6 months - 3 years**, with zone, duties and fee share written in | VPO Master Plan 2022, Figs 11-8/11-9 - `pdf-reports/` |
| **A5. Registration + permits** | Contractor takes NEMC permits (fees: TZS 50,000 per application; up to TZS 5,000,000 for >5-tonne operations), registers the workplace (OSHA ss.15-20), registers workers (NSSF 10% + WCF ~0.6%), incorporates (BRELA) and holds a business licence | GN 387/2021 fee schedule; OSHA Act 2003; NEMC forms in `sample-documents/` |

### PHASE B - DAILY OPERATION AND MONEY FLOW (repeats until the contract ends)

| Step | What happens | The rule / proof |
|---|---|---|
| **B6. Household/business pays RCC** | Residential **TZS 1,150-2,300/month** (2021); market band: households 2,000-15,000; shops 10,000-30,000; hotels up to 300,000. Payment compliance only 30-50% in low-income areas | CSE 2021; VPO 2022 |
| **B7. Contractor collects** | Roadworthy vehicle with inspection certificate (GN 177/2001 Reg 3); PPE for every worker (OSHA 2003 s.62); collection per the zone schedule | Laws in `laws-and-regulations/` |
| **B8. Money split - 3 real models** | **Model 1 (Kinondoni/Ubungo):** contractor keeps **50-100%** of RCC, remits 0-50%. **Model 2 (Ilala):** contractor deposits **100%** into the council account, council deducts the commission (often ~10%) and remits the rest. **Model 3 (Kinondoni 2024):** council PAYS providers per verified service (results-based) | VPO 2022 Fig 11-10; Ilomo & Charles 2025; Kinondoni tender 108016059 |
| **B9. Disposal at Pugu** | Truck pays the **gate fee per tonne** (1,500-2,000 TZS/t household; 8,000-15,000 TZS/t industrial/special) and gets a **weighbridge receipt + tonnage record** | CSE 2021; GN 263/2009 Reg 9(5); Down To Earth 2023 |
| **B10. Report, audit, renew** | Contractor keeps records every 6 months (Reg 60); LGA files an annual report incl. copies of permits (Reg 53); CAG audits (found un-banked money and missing bonds); contract renewed or terminated | GN 263/2009 Regs 53/60; CAG AG Reports 2021/22 + 2022/23 |

**Where the money actually went (real audit data):** Ubungo disbursed **TZS 2,552,694,558.96**
of RCC to contractors/CBOs in one year; Temeke collected **TZS 3,790,711,379** of refuse fees
in FY2022/23; Ilala agents **under-banked TZS 285.89M** and under-collected **TZS 1.55B**
(CAG 2022/23). These are exactly the gaps Phase II's digital platform and the new public
company are designed to close.

## PART 2 - DISTRICT BY DISTRICT: CONTRACTORS, FEES, ACHIEVEMENTS, PROBLEMS

### 2.1 ILALA / Dar es Salaam City Council - the pioneer

**Real contractors documented (each has a profile file + source PDF in `districts/01_ILALA_DCC/`):**

| Contractor | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Green WastePro Ltd** | Smart-TRACS pilot in Kisutu + Mchafukoge: **+129% service users, +13.5% revenue, +55% payment coverage** (Pellatt & Palfreman 2023, GeoJournal) |
| **Magomeni Taka Group** | est. 2021, 30 members; **TZS 200,000,000 interest-free municipal loan** for a collection truck (Ilomo & Charles 2025) |
| **Mandela Group** | est. 2012, 12 widows + youth; ward contract |
| **Vingunguti Group** | est. 2007, 9 adults; ward contract |
| **Peramiho Group** | est. 2016, 12 members; ward contract |
| **Wakusanya Taka Bonyokwa Cooperative** | collects **1.74 t/day** with Nipe Fagio (GAIA case study) |

**Fee package:** contractor collects RCC and **deposits the whole amount** into the council
account; council deducts the contractual commission (**often ~10%**) and remits the rest.
Real quote (Ilomo & Charles 2025): a group pays **TZS 525,000/month = 10% of estimated
TZS 5.25M monthly collections**.

**Achieved:** first privatization in Tanzania (**1994**, 5-year contract over 11 wards);
first documented smart contractor pilot; 5 documented grassroots ward groups.

**Problems:** CAG 2022/23 found refuse-agent contracts **without valid bonds**; agents
**under-banked TZS 285.89M** and **under-collected TZS 1.55B**; waste piles in Tabata and
Kinyerezi; some groups did NOT deposit the collected fees (Ilomo Table 2); no collection
points (CAG plastic audit).

### 2.2 KINONDONI - the template system

**Real contractors:** the **17 named cleansing contractors** (Kamugisha 2019, Appendix III -
the only published district roster in the country): A.J.M COMPANY LTD, BAM WASTE MANAGEMENT,
ENEA GROUP, KURO ENVIRONMENTAL CARE LTD, GORDIAN Z. KIKARUGA, JUHUDI COOPORATION, KIFUNA
HERSHODAN INTER.CO, KINONDONI ENVIRONMENTALIST, KIWODET, UUGAI NENGA, LYCAM INVESTMENT,
LUGOLI ENTERPRISEE, MNEMVU TRADERS, QUALITY SYSTEMS (T), SKY MARS SERVICES, T.T.M. GROUP,
ZUBISH ENTERPRISES; plus the **Bunju Group** (grassroots, documented loan-rejection case).

**Fee package:** PPP running **since 1995**; contractor **keeps 50-100%** of RCC and remits
0-50% to council. Official tender **LGA017/2020-21** (8 lots, disposal at Pugu). **2024
results-based tender** (Ref Id 108016059): the council pays providers per verified service.

**Achieved:** best collection rate in the city (**65.0%**, 2017); its 2000 by-laws became the
national template; only district with a published named contractor roster.

**Problems:** 76% of households had **no door-to-door service** (Mnyanyi 2014); Mabwepande
compost plant at **10-25 t/day vs 50 t capacity** after a TZS 7.1B project (CAG 2022/23);
no collection points; 2024 tender winners never published.

### 2.3 UBUNGO - the contractor economy

**Real contractors/data:** **contractors + CBOs do 47%** of all collection (council itself:
4%); documented award **Umoja Wetu ni Ushindi Wetu, TZS 70,000,000** (King'ngo St, Saranga
ward - single-source award notice, flagged). The UMC contractor registry exists but is **not
published** - stated honestly, not guessed.

**Fee package:** RCC flows through **mtaa executive officers**: **TZS 2,552,694,558.96**
disbursed to contractors/CBOs in one year; UMC SWM budget TZS 1,542,565,841.92 (2020-21);
same keep-share model as Kinondoni (adopted the 2000 by-laws).

**Achieved:** best-documented contractor economy; **2024 contracts signed** (~16,005 t
collected in April 2024 per council announcement); ward-by-ward tonnage published for all
14 wards.

**Problems:** **49% not collected**; infrastructure shortage (CAG Tables 108-109);
**unauthorized dumpsite at Goba Majengo / Tegeta River** (CAG 2022/23 p.171).

### 2.4 TEMEKE - hardest start, ward-level progress

**Real contractors:** 2016 - only **ONE formal contractor city-wide** (municipal officer,
Millinga 2016 sec. 4.5.1). In **Mbagala ward**, the municipality contracted 3 groups and
scored them (Magombola 2021):

| Group | Capacity Performance Index |
|---|---|
| **Kimuchi Group** | **84% = A+ (Level 3)** |
| **Kisonge Group** | **66% = B+ (Level 2)** |
| **Orphan & Widow Group** | **47% = C (Forming-storming)** |

**Fee package:** refuse fees collected FY2022/23 = **TZS 3,790,711,379** (CAG App 46, p.355);
own by-laws GN 45/2001 allow any number of council-appointed agents; **2022 tender** to lease
the Mtoni landfill to a private operator (PPRA TPJ Vol IX Ed 29).

**Achieved:** own gazetted by-laws since 2001; 3 real ward groups under municipal contract
with CPI scoring; revenue now in the billions per year.

**Problems:** 2013 - collected only **280 of 743 t/day**, 8 of 10 council lorries worn out;
one group performing at CPI 47%; Mtoni landfill still needs its private operator (award not
published). **About the "7 contractors" claim:** no source gives Temeke 7 contractors - the
documents show 1 city-wide formal contractor (2016) + ward-level CBO groups.

### 2.5 KIGAMBONI - newest, building the market

**Real contractors:** **Taka Ajira** operator with documented bid tiers **TZS 4,000 /
8,000 / 15,000 per month** (415-household survey, Lundumen 2022). Market roster from the
same study: **Green Waste Pro, Tirima, Eco Protection** (the 3 largest) + More4less, Wejisa,
Environmental Solution and Facility, Gin Investment, BP Environment, Recovery LTD = **9 named
companies**.

**Fee package:** documented willingness-to-pay tiers 4,000/8,000/15,000; no tender award
ever published - the market was built by private operators; Vijibweni FSTP project brief 2024
(DAWASA/Ministry of Water).

**Achieved:** 9 named private companies active; Taka Ajira created tiered pricing citizens
actually accepted; new faecal-sludge infrastructure planned at Vijibweni.

**Problems:** lowest collection (**33.3%**, 2017); **Lingato dumping site unfenced, no
controls** (CAG 2022/23 App 47, p.356); plastic waste piles near the Ferry (CAG photo).

## PART 3 - THE PROBLEMS CONTRACTORS FACE (all documented)

1. **Payment delays** - majority of LGAs take **at least 30 days** to pay service providers (VPO 2022 Fig 11-7).
2. **Short contracts** - 6 months to 3 years; too short to justify truck investment (VPO 2022).
3. **RCC too low** - "considered too low by private contractors" (CSE 2021).
4. **Non-payment by citizens** - 30-50% payment compliance in low-income areas (CSE 2021); 23.02% of households do not participate (IIARI).
5. **Audit findings against agents** - contracts without valid bonds; TZS 285.89M under-banked (CAG 2022/23, Ilala).
6. **Commission disputes** - some councils refused commissions below target (Ilomo & Charles 2025).
7. **Worker safety** - 50% of workers untrained, 89% feel at risk (REPOA n=431); Pugu pickers injured: 70% of men, 60% of women (WB PID); PPE duty exists (OSHA s.62) but enforcement is weak.
8. **System behind it all** - only ~36% of 5,733 t/day collected (3,692 t uncollected daily); ~1% recovery; open dumpsite instead of sanitary landfill.

### 4.2 ILLUSTRATIVE scenario (clearly labeled - NOT real data)

A contractor with a zone of **2,000 households at TZS 3,000/month** (mid-market fee, inside
the real band):

| Line | Math | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Billed over 36 months | 2,000 x 3,000 x 36 | **TZS 216,000,000** |
| Collected at 65% compliance (real compliance range 30-50% low-income, higher mid-income) | 216M x 0.65 | **~TZS 140,400,000** |
| Ilala model - council commission 10% | 140.4M x 0.10 = 14.04M | contractor nets **~TZS 126,360,000** |
| Kinondoni/Ubungo model - keep 70% (mid of 50-100%) | 140.4M x 0.70 | contractor keeps **~TZS 98,280,000** + remits 42M |
| Same zone on a 6-month contract | 216M x 0.5/6 = 18M billed | 6x more renewal risk, no truck financing |

**Read the honest label:** the per-contractor scenario is arithmetic on the real fee bands;
the zone size is hypothetical. The district-scale numbers above are real audit data.
A 3-year contract at these bands is why contractors want longer deals; the observed real
money at district scale (TZS 2.5-3.8 billion/year) shows the sector already moves billions.

|---|---|

## PART 7 - DAR ES SALAAM IN ONE PARAGRAPH (the general finalization)

Dar es Salaam generates **5,733 tonnes of waste every day** and collects only about **36%**,
leaving **3,692 t/day** uncollected, with ~1% recovery. Five districts run five variations of
the same RCC-contractor machine: Kinondoni (65% collected, 17 named contractors, results-based
tender), Ilala/DCC (55%, deposit-all + ~10% commission, the smart pilot), Ubungo (51%,
47% contractor/CBO share, TZS 2.55B/year flowing), Temeke (45%, ward groups scored by CPI,
TZS 3.79B/year collected), Kigamboni (33%, 9 private companies, tiered Taka Ajira pricing).
The shared problems are payments (30+ day delays), short contracts (6-36 months), citizen
non-payment, weak records, and an open dumpsite with ~500 unprotected pickers. DMDP Phase I
(2015-2023) built the roads, flood defenses, settlements and the digital revenue base but
left waste infrastructure for later - honestly. DMDP Phase II (2024-) is the answer: US$117M
to build the sanitary landfills, transfer stations, recycling and composting plants, close
Pugu, and put the shared facilities under a public company owned by all five districts,
operated through private contracts - the same contractors documented in this pack, finally
with a system worth contracting into.

## PART 6b - PHASE II STATUS CHECK: HAS IT ACTUALLY ACHIEVED ANYTHING? (verified)

> Short answer: **YES - real money committed and first contracts on the ground in Year 1
> (2024-25), plus an emergency flood response. But the waste infrastructure - the flagship
> part - is still on paper.** Everything below is verified against the World Bank
> Restructuring Paper in this pack (`pdf-reports/WB_PAD_2024_DSM_ClimateResilience.pdf`,
> reference A3) and cross-checked with Tanzanian press coverage (references G5-G7).

### What Phase II HAS achieved (as of the latest official data, late 2024 into 2025)

| # | Achievement | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Project legally live.** Approved Dec 20, 2023; **effective May 27, 2024**; financing locked: **US$385M IDA credit + US$53.02M Netherlands grant = US$438M total** | Restructuring Paper para 3; PID financing data (A2, A3 in `07_References.md`) |
| 2 | **US$168M (43% of the loan) already committed to construction.** **17 road upgrading contracts awarded October 2024**; works **began January 2025**, completion target October 2025. Press: 8 contracts signed covering 168 km; four Chinese contractors among the winners (e.g. Jiaxing Geo-Engineering, 13.85 km at TZS 47.7 billion) | Restructuring Paper para 7; thechanzo.com 24-Oct-2024 + Guardian/Daily News 8-Nov-2024 (G5, G6) |
| 3 | **Official ratings good.** World Bank rated DMDP2 **"Satisfactory" toward the PDO and "Satisfactory" on Implementation Progress**; procurement (PRAMS) rated Satisfactory Oct 3, 2024; **no overdue financial audits** | Restructuring Paper para 8 |
| 4 | **The two new governance bodies moved forward** - the metropolitan **steering committee** and the **solid waste technical committee** were being formalized on the agreed covenant timeline | Restructuring Paper para 8 |
| 5 | **Emergency response actually delivered.** CERC **activated Sep 3, 2024: US$65M** reallocated to repair flood-damaged transport infrastructure after the April 2024 floods + Cyclone Hidaya (recovery needs est. US$630M = 0.82% of GDP); restructuring approved to formalize it | Restructuring Paper paras 1, 4-9 |
| 6 | **Phase I legacy carried in.** Phase I (P123134) closed Jan 2, 2023 with 4.1M beneficiaries, 207.8 km roads, 67 km stormwater drains, 406 ha flood-protected land, 152 settlements upgraded - the institutional and digital-revenue base Phase II builds on | WB PID P123134 at closure; Connective Cities good-practice note (June 2025, G8) |

### What Phase II has NOT achieved yet (the honest half)

| Fact | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Every waste-infrastructure indicator was still at 0** at the March 18, 2024 results check: landfill m3, sorting/recycling/composting t/day, dumpsite ha closed, waste pickers enrolled | Restructuring Paper results framework |
| The waste targets are **for later in the project window**: 45 t/day sorting/recycling/composting capacity, **80 ha of dumpsites closed**, livelihoods for the ~**450-500 Pugu pickers**, **5 LGAs** with operational service-improvement plans - all closing **Dec 2029/Jan 2030** | Restructuring Paper results framework |
| Designs for the remaining investments were **still being completed during 2025**; landfill, transfer-station and composting contracts come after that | Restructuring Paper para 7 |
| The **inter-municipal public company** (the new owner-operator of the shared facilities) is **not yet operational** - only its steering/technical committees were in formation | Restructuring Paper para 8 |
| The CERC activation **created a US$65M financing gap** that still has to be refilled by Additional Financing | Restructuring Paper para 6 |

### Verdict in one paragraph

Phase II is **not** a paper project - in its first year it signed US$168M of works (43% of
the loan), got "Satisfactory" ratings on both PDO and implementation, moved its new
metropolitan governance bodies forward, and actually paid for flood repairs through the
CERC. But **the waste story - sanitary landfills, closing Pugu, the 5-district public
company - is exactly where it was promised and not where it is delivered**: zero waste
infrastructure indicators had moved by the March 2024 results check, waste designs were
finishing through 2025, and the 2024-2030 closing date puts the big waste milestones
(45 t/day recycling, 80 ha dumpsite closure, 450+ pickers re-livelihooded) at the end of
the project, not the start. So: **roads and flood response = achieved; the waste
revolution = funded and contracted to happen, not yet built.**
